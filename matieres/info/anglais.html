Passive Voice

Rule : Object becomes Subject + verb to be (in the tense of the active verb) + Past Participle (P.P.) (+ by + agent if needed).

  • Present Simple : They build eco-houses. → Eco-houses are built.
  • Past Simple : Edison invented the light bulb. → The light bulb was invented by Edison.
  • Present Perfect : Scientists have found a cure. → A cure has been found by scientists.
  • Modals / Future : We must protect nature. → Nature must be protected.

Reported Speech (Indirect Speech)

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told, asked), shift tenses one step back and adjust pronouns and time/place markers:

⏪ Tense Backshift

  • Present Simple → Past Simple
  • Present Continuous → Past Continuous
  • Past Simple / Present Perfect → Past Perfect
  • will / can / may / must → would / could / might / had to

🕒 Time & Place Changes

  • now → then  |  today → that day
  • yesterday → the day before
  • tomorrow → the next / following day
  • here → there  |  this / these → that / those
  • Yes/No Questions : She asked, "Do you like virtual schools?" → She asked me if / whether I liked virtual schools.
  • Wh- Questions : He asked, "Where did you go?" → He asked me where I had gone. (No inversion!)
  • Commands / Advice : "Don't waste water!" → She told us not to waste water.

Conditionals & Regret

TypeStructureMeaning / Example

Type 1If + Present Simple, will + VReal / likely future: If you revise, you will succeed.
Type 2If + Past Simple, would + VUnreal present: If I were rich, I would travel.
Type 3If + Past Perfect, would have + P.P.Unreal past / regret: If he had studied, he would have passed.
  • Unless = If ... not : Unless you work hard, you won't succeed. (= If you do not work hard...)
  • Expressing Regret : I wish / If only + Past Perfect (about the past) — I wish I had listened to my teacher. Use Past Simple for a wish about the present.

Modals & Key Functions

Obligation / Prohibition
must / have to (obligation), mustn't (prohibition), don't have to / needn't (lack of obligation).
Advice
should / shouldn't / ought to / had better + V.
Deduction
must + V (almost certain it is true), can't + V (certain it is impossible), may / might / could + V (possibility). Past deduction: modal + have + P.P.
Used to
used to + V (past habit no longer true) vs. be / get used to + V-ing / noun (be accustomed to).

Writing Guide (15 Points)

Guided Writing (5 Points)

Turn notes, a biography, or a table/chart into a coherent paragraph of 5–6 sentences:

  • Use every single piece of information given in the prompt (dates, numbers, facts).
  • Use the right tense (usually Past Simple for biographies/past data, Present Simple for general facts).
  • Connect sentences smoothly with linkers (First, Moreover, However, In addition, Finally).

Free Writing (10 Points) — Essay Structure

  1. Introduction (3–4 lines)General hook about the topic → narrow down to the issue → clear thesis statement.
  2. Body Paragraph 1 (5–6 lines)Clear topic sentence + 2 supporting arguments with concrete examples.
  3. Body Paragraph 2 (5–6 lines)Second topic sentence (further arguments, opposite viewpoint, or practical solutions) + examples.
  4. Conclusion (3–4 lines)Summarize main points without repeating exact sentences + final thought, recommendation, or call to action.

Exam Checklist (Bac English)

Final 5-minute check
  1. Reading : always answer in your own words when asked, and check whether a True/False justification must be copied as an exact sentence from the text.
  2. Language : read the entire sentence before filling a blank — check subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and whether a negative prefix is needed.
  3. Writing : spare 5 minutes at the end to proofread for 3rd-person -s, irregular past verbs, spelling, and punctuation.
